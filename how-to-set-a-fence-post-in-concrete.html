How to set a fence post in concrete

A fence is only as straight as its posts. Getting the hole size right and choosing the right concrete method matters more than how much concrete you pour.

Quick answer

Dig a hole about 3 times the width of the post and about one-third of the post's total length deep, plus 6 inches for gravel. For a 4x4 post on a 6 ft fence, that is a hole about 10 to 12 inches wide and roughly 30 to 36 inches deep (deeper where frost goes deeper). Add 6 inches of gravel, set the post plumb, and fill with concrete to just below ground level. Fast-setting mix sets in about 20 to 40 minutes; wait at least 4 hours before hanging rails, and about a day or more with regular concrete mix.

Hole size rules

Width

A common rule from concrete makers is a hole about 3 times the post's width. A 4x4 post actually measures 3 1/2 inches, so the hole should be about 10 to 12 inches across. A 6x6 (5 1/2 inches actual) needs about 16 to 18 inches. In loose or sandy soil, go wider at the bottom.

Depth

Bury about one-third of the post's length, then add 6 inches for the gravel layer.

In cold climates, the bottom of the post should sit below the local frost line so freezing soil does not heave it upward. Frost depths range from a few inches in the Deep South to 4 ft or more in the northern states; your local building department can tell you yours. Gate posts carry more load, so many builders dig them wider and deeper than line posts.

Pouring dry works because fast-setting mix is designed to pull in water. For holes deeper than about 2 1/2 ft, add the mix in stages: pour part, add water, let it soak in, then repeat. Regular concrete mix should not be dumped dry and watered in the hole; it needs to be fully mixed to reach its rated strength.

How many bags per post

Concrete volume = hole volume - post volume - gravel layer. For a 10 inch round hole with 24 inches of concrete around a 4x4:

  • Hole: 3.14 x 5 x 5 x 24 = about 1,885 cubic inches
  • Post: 3.5 x 3.5 x 24 = 294 cubic inches
  • Concrete: about 1,590 cubic inches, or 0.92 cu ft

That is about 2 1/2 bags of 50 lb fast-setting mix (buy 3) or about 1 1/2 bags of 80 lb regular mix (buy 2). The fence post concrete calculator runs this for any hole size and number of posts.

Step by step

  1. Call 811 to have utilities marked before digging.
  2. Mark post locations with a string line between corner posts. Line posts are usually 6 to 8 ft apart; match your panel or rail length.
  3. Dig the holes with a post-hole digger or auger. Keep the sides straight and the bottom slightly wider than the top if possible.
  4. Add gravel. Put about 6 inches of gravel or crushed stone in the bottom and tamp it. This drains water away from the end of the post.
  5. Set the post on the gravel. Check plumb on two adjacent faces with a level and brace it with two stakes and scrap boards.
  6. Add concrete to about 3 to 4 inches below ground level (fast-setting) or near grade (regular mix).
  7. Shape the top. Slope the concrete surface away from the post so water runs off. You can cover the top later with soil or gravel.
  8. Recheck plumb in the first few minutes, before the concrete sets.
  9. Wait before attaching rails or panels: at least 4 hours for fast-setting, about 1 to 2 days for regular mix.

Tips for posts that last

  • Use wood rated for ground contact. Ordinary treated lumber rated only for above-ground use rots faster in the hole.
  • Do not pour a concrete pad under the post. A closed bottom traps water against the end grain.
  • Set corner and end posts first, then pull a string between them to align line posts.
  • In hot weather, fast-setting mix sets faster, so have everything ready before adding water.
