How it's calculated

The calculator counts blocks course by course, rounding up so each row is complete.

Blocks per course = wall length (in) / block face length (in), rounded up
Courses = exposed height (in) / block face height (in), rounded up, plus 1 buried course
Total blocks = blocks per course x courses
Caps = wall length (in) / cap length (in), rounded up

Example: a wall 20 ft long and 2 ft high built from 4 x 11.75 in blocks needs 240 / 11.75 = 20.4, so 21 blocks per course. Two feet is 24 inches, or 6 courses, plus 1 buried course makes 7. That is 147 blocks and 20 caps of about 12 inches.

Gravel is estimated for a drainage zone 12 inches deep behind the full block height and a leveling pad 6 inches deep and 24 inches wide. Use clean 3/4 in angular stone behind the wall and a compactable base under it. Block sizes differ by brand, so measure the face of the block you will buy.

Common questions

How many retaining wall blocks do I need?

Divide the wall length by the block length for blocks per course, then multiply by the number of courses. A 20 ft wall of 4 x 11.75 in blocks, 2 ft high plus a buried course, needs about 147 blocks.

How deep should the first course be buried?

A common guideline is to bury at least one full course, or about 1 inch for every 8 inches of wall height, whichever is more. Follow your block maker's instructions.

Do I need gravel behind a retaining wall?

Yes. About 12 inches of clean angular stone behind the blocks lets water drain instead of building pressure that pushes the wall over.

How tall can a block retaining wall be without reinforcement?

Small garden blocks are usually limited to about 2 to 3 feet. Many codes require engineering or a permit for walls over 4 feet.
